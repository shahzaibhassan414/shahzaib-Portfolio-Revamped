**H1:** Paw Play Love: a social app for pet owners, built in Flutter

Paw Play Love helps pet owners find playdates and other pet people nearby. It works like a dating app for pets: swipe through profiles, match, and chat in real time. Premium members get boosts, super likes, and rewinds.

### LawnOlu

LawnOlu connects customers with lawn-care providers. Customers book a service, track the provider live on a map, pay, and get updates. Providers manage their job queue, navigate to customers, and get paid.

I built both the customer and provider experiences in one Flutter codebase, with role-based navigation and state so each user only sees what applies to them. The challenge was keeping the UI responsive while audio and live data changed constantly. I structured the listeners so only the widgets that needed new data rebuilt, which kept sessions smooth.

**Stack:** Flutter, Firebase Realtime Database, real-time audio streaming, GetX

### The product

Most dating apps are profile catalogs: you swipe through strangers who could be anywhere. On Scene starts from the opposite end. It shows you where people are right now, which venues are busy tonight, and who just checked in near you, so meeting someone is a matter of going somewhere rather than messaging for a week.

### The problem

The app had to connect online dating with real-world presence. That meant a map that always reflected what was actually happening, check-ins that registered instantly, live updates for everyone nearby, private chat, and a premium tier, all in one app that still felt fast on an average Android phone.

### What I built

- **Live presence** that updates who's around a venue as people arrive and leave
- **Real-time chat** over Socket.IO, with Firebase push notifications for messages and activity
- **Matching** between people at or near the same venues

### Technical challenges

**Keeping a busy map smooth.** Map screens get slow quickly when markers, location updates, and socket events all arrive at once. Every presence update can trigger a rebuild, and a naive approach redraws the entire map for each one. I structured the location and real-time logic so the map stayed responsive as activity changed around the user.

**Presence that stays accurate.** People close the app, lose signal, or walk out of a venue. Presence has to expire properly rather than showing someone who left an hour ago.

**Subscriptions on two stores.** Premium access has to unlock right after purchase, survive reinstalls and device changes, and behave identically on Apple and Google.

### Important engineering decisions

1. **Venues first, profiles second.** Discovery starts on the map, which is what makes On Scene different from a standard swipe app.
2. **Sockets for presence, not polling.** Socket events tied to map presence keep nearby activity current without constant API requests.
3. **RevenueCat for both stores.** One source of truth for subscription status and entitlement checks across Apple and Google billing, instead of two hand-built integrations.

## 10. Case study: BePay

**URL:** /work/bepay · **H1:** BePay: Web3 commerce apps for shoppers and merchants, built in Flutter

### Overview

BePay is a Web3 commerce platform with two Flutter apps: BePay Client for shoppers and BePay Business for merchants. Shoppers pay with a crypto wallet created from a familiar Google or Apple login. Merchants verify their business and manage products and orders from their phone. I built the mobile side of both.

### The product

Crypto payments usually lose people at the first step: install a wallet, write down a seed phrase, buy tokens, connect. BePay's idea is to make paying with a wallet feel like any other checkout, for shoppers who don't think of themselves as crypto users and merchants who just want to get paid.

### The problem

Two audiences with very different needs. Shoppers need onboarding that feels like a normal sign-up, plus identity verification that doesn't feel like paperwork. Merchants need business verification (KYB), product and listing management, and order operations. Both apps mix ordinary REST APIs with blockchain-backed operations, and wallet data has to stay secure on the device.

### My role

Flutter developer for both apps: wallet onboarding, KYC and KYB flows, REST API integration, merchant tools, and secure handling of wallet-related data. \[CONTENT NEEDED FROM SHAHZAIB: team size, timeline, and whether these were built at Code Encoders.\]

### What I built

**BePay Client (shoppers)**

- Wallet creation through Web3Auth using Google or Apple sign-in, with no seed phrase on day one
- KYC verification screens using DIDIT
- Hybrid commerce flows combining REST APIs with blockchain-backed payments

**BePay Business (merchants)**

- Multi-step KYB verification for business onboarding and compliance
- Product, listing, and order management with search and filters
- Operational screens for running Web3 commerce from a phone

### Technical challenges

**Making wallets feel normal.** Every extra step in Web3 onboarding loses users. Using Web3Auth meant a wallet could be created through an OAuth login people already trust.

**Keeping Web2 and Web3 apart.** Mixing API calls and blockchain operations in the same layer makes a codebase fragile and hard to test.

**Verification that adapts.** KYB requirements vary by business, so the onboarding form couldn't be hard-coded.

**Protecting wallet data.** Session and wallet-related metadata had to be stored securely on the device.

### Important engineering decisions

1. **Repository layer split by concern.** Web2 REST logic and Web3 wallet operations live in separate repositories, so each can change without breaking the other.
2. **Dynamic, multi-step KYB forms.** The form structure is driven by requirements rather than fixed screens, so new verification rules don't need new UI.
3. **Secure on-device storage** for wallet-related metadata and sessions.
4. **Provider for merchant state** across KYB, products, listings, and orders, with pagination-ready lists for merchants with large catalogs.

### Technology stack

Flutter, Dart, Web3Auth, DIDIT KYC and KYB SDKs, REST APIs, Provider, secure storage

### Outcome

BePay gave shoppers a wallet in a single social login and gave merchants a mobile back office for verification, listings, and orders.

### What I learned

In fintech, the hard part is trust, not code. Every screen in onboarding either builds confidence or creates doubt, and good architecture is what lets you change those screens quickly when compliance rules change.
